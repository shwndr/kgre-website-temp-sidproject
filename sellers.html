A Seller's Guide, Step by Step.

Everything you need to know about selling a home with Kevin Grimes Sr. and the Greatness Real Estate Team — organized by stage.

Decide to Sell

So you've decided to sell your property. Before anything else, it's a good idea to sit down and clarify your motivations, and draw up a basic time frame for your ideal selling process.

Why Sell?

Why do you want to sell your property? Do you intend to simply find a larger property, or do you plan on moving to another neighborhood, school district, city, or state? You might think your reasons are obvious, but it helps to consider the implications of each option for your lifestyle, opportunities, and finances. Being clear about your intentions for selling makes it easier for Kevin to determine the most appropriate option for your financial, lifestyle, and real estate goals.

When Should I Sell?

The first thing you should do is establish your time frame for selling. If you need to sell quickly, Kevin can speed up the process with a complete market analysis and action plan to reach your goals. If there's no pressing need to sell immediately, you can sit down with Kevin to thoroughly review current market conditions and find the most favorable time to sell.

What Is the Market Like?

When you work with Kevin, you have his knowledge, expertise, and negotiating skills working for you to arrive at the best market price and terms. He'll keep you up to date on what's happening in the marketplace — the price, financing, terms, and conditions of competing properties — so you know exactly how to price, and when to sell, your property.

How Do I Optimize My Finances?

Deciding to sell your property demands a serious look at your current financial situation and future plans. With Kevin's help, you'll be able to assess the impact of these changes, estimate the potential proceeds of selling your property, and plan effective tax and estate strategies — so you take full control of your finances.

What to Look For in an Agent.

A few factors to keep in mind when choosing a listing agent:

  1. Experience and expertise. You want a full-time agent who is familiar with your area and the type of property you intend to sell. Does the agent use a range of marketing and advertising strategies? How tech-savvy are they? How many similar properties have they sold?
  2. Availability and commitment. Your agent should be capable of prompt, decisive action throughout the sale. Do they keep in touch with you consistently? Can they be reached in an emergency, or for the simplest questions? Are they available evenings and weekends, when most buyers are out looking?
  3. Rapport. Does your agent take the time to listen to your goals and understand your needs? Your listing agent is your guide and partner in this decision, so it matters that you can work well together.

Prepare to Sell

You can do a lot to increase the appeal of your property and make a lasting impression on potential buyers.

What to Do to Prepare

A few factors to keep in mind when preparing your property for sale:

  1. Curb Appeal. Keeping your landscape pristine, and adding creative touches to your yard such as colorful annuals, creates an immediate impact on passers-by and potential buyers.
  2. Property Repairs. Simple upgrades such as window repairs, polished doorknobs, and a fresh coat of paint in the most-used rooms instantly brighten up a property.
  3. Cleanliness and Staging. Keep your property uncluttered, fresh-smelling, and well-lit from top to bottom. Details matter — put away the pet supplies, place fresh flowers near the entryway, have the carpets cleaned. Kevin will walk the property before it's listed to help you plan the staging.
  4. Disclosures and Inspections. Kevin is familiar with the legal procedures involved in disclosures and will help you develop a thorough disclosure statement that protects both you and the buyer, as well as suggest inspections (such as termite and pest) before listing.
  5. Showtime. Presenting your property to potential buyers is handled for you — from professional photography to a virtual 3D walkthrough where appropriate.

Accepting an Offer

The Price Is Not Always Right.

"The higher the price, the better the offer" — don't let that popular misconception fool you. Price isn't always the deciding factor when accepting an offer: an initial offer usually isn't final, and there are terms and conditions that can influence the final outcome. Trust Kevin's experience to help you evaluate every proposal thoroughly, without compromising your position.

Negotiating the Right Way.

Kevin takes the responsibility of negotiating contractual terms fairly and seriously — the goal is a win-win agreement that works for everyone involved. You may have to weigh multiple offers before choosing the one that's right for you, and Kevin will give each one a thorough, objective assessment to help you make the right call.

The Initial Agreement and Deposit.

An effective agreement is a legal arrangement between a potential purchaser and the property's seller. Laws vary by state, but for the agreement to be legally binding, it typically requires consideration — an initial and additional deposit — held in the closing agent's escrow account pending the fulfillment of the agreement's conditions or contingencies.

Some important tips to keep the process moving smoothly:

  • Keep written records of everything. Kevin will help draft all the paperwork for your sale and make sure you have copies of everything.
  • Stick to the schedule. Once you've chosen an offer, you and the buyer will be given a timeline marking every stage of closing. Meeting requirements on time keeps negotiations moving and keeps both parties in compliance with the agreement — Kevin will keep you updated throughout, so you're always prepared for the next step.

Escrow, Inspections and Appraisals

Inspections and Appraisals

Most buyers will have the property inspected by a licensed property inspector within the time frame agreed upon in the contract to purchase. Some buyers bring in several inspectors to get professional opinions on a specific area (e.g. roof, HVAC, structure). If the agreement is conditional upon financing, the property will be appraised by a licensed appraiser to determine its value for the lending institution, via a third party — confirming their investment in the property is accurate. A buyer of a commercial property may also require a complete environmental audit and/or soil test, if the lending institution requires it.

The Closing Agent.

Either a title company or an attorney will be selected as the closing agent, whose job is to examine and insure clear title to the property. After researching the property's complete recorded history, they'll certify that your title is free and clear of encumbrances (such as mortgages, leases, restrictions, or liens) by the date of closing, and that any new encumbrances are properly included in the title.

Contingencies.

A contingency is a condition that must be met before a contract becomes legally binding — for instance, buyers often include a contingency stating their contract is binding only once there's a satisfactory home inspection report from a qualified inspector.

Before completing the purchase, the buyer reviews every aspect of the property as provided for in the purchase agreement and any addendums, including:

  • Obtaining financing and insurance
  • Reviewing all pertinent documents, such as preliminary title reports and disclosure documents
  • Inspecting the property — buyers can subject it to a range of inspections, such as roof, termite/pest, chimney/fireplace, property boundary survey, well, septic, pool/spa, arborist, mold, lead-based paint, and HVAC

Depending on the outcome of these inspections, one of two things may happen:

  1. Each milestone is successfully closed and the contingencies are removed, bringing you one step closer to closing; or
  2. The buyer, after reviewing the property and the paperwork, requests a renegotiation of the terms of the contract (usually the price).

Responding objectively and fairly to a renegotiation request — while protecting your interests — is exactly where an experienced listing agent makes a real difference. Kevin has handled property sales in every kind of situation, and brings that experience and commitment to every client.

Loan Approval and Appraisal.

Kevin recommends accepting buyers who have a lender's pre-approval, approval letter, or written loan commitment — a stronger guarantee of loan approval than a pre-qualification or no documentation at all. Expect an appraiser from the lender's company to review your property and confirm the sales price is appropriate.

Close of Escrow

Closing Day

If you've come this far, it's almost time for a congratulations — but not quite yet. Don't forget to tie up these loose ends:

Be Prepared.

Kevin is ready to assist you should an unforeseen glitch pop up, even at this last stage. If something at the property breaks down, or the buyer's loan doesn't come through on time, there's no need to worry — Kevin has encountered these problems before, so he knows how to handle them efficiently and in a stress-free manner.

Closing.

The closing agent will furnish all parties with a settlement statement, which summarizes and details the financial transactions in the process. The buyer(s) will sign this statement, and then you'll sign as well, along with the closing agent, certifying its accuracy. If you're unable to attend the scheduled closing, arrangements can be made depending on the circumstances and the notice given. If you're receiving funds from the transaction, you can elect to either have the funds wired electronically to your financial institution, or have a check issued to you at closing.
